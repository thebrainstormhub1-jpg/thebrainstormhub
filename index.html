Archive Entry — 1968 CE

The City That
Broke Biafra's Back

Two sides. One city. A war that split a nation in two.

SCROLL TO READ

By September 1968, the Nigerian Civil War had ground into its second year, and Biafra's secessionist government was running out of room. Federal commander Benjamin Adekunle had boasted he could take Owerri, Aba, and Umuahia — Biafra's three remaining major cities — in two weeks. He was wrong about the timeline, but not the target.

Movement I — Two Cities Left

Aba was Biafra's administrative capital and its largest surviving city, a commercial hub of roughly 100,000 people. It stood alongside Owerri and Umuahia as one of the last three major cities still under Biafran control — and federal forces had made clear it wouldn't stand for long.

Movement II — The Siege

Federal forces spent August cutting off food supplies into the city, a strategy to surround and starve it out before the final assault. Aba held on under heavy shelling for as long as it could.

Movement III — The City Falls

On September 4, 1968, Aba fell — its population fleeing as federal troops entered a city they found largely deserted. The loss was catastrophic. Biafran-held territory shrank to roughly 5,000 square miles, about one-sixth of the secessionist state's original size. With Aba gone, only Owerri and Umuahia remained under Biafran control, and the "Biafran heartland" kept shrinking by the week.

Movement IV — Defiance

But the story didn't end there. Biafran forces regrouped with remarkable resilience. They launched a fierce counter-attack and recaptured Aba just weeks later on October 15, 1968, before successfully retaking Owerri the following April — a reminder that even when pushed to the brink, Biafra wasn't finished fighting. The fall of Aba wasn't just a battlefield loss; it was a preview of the defiance that would keep Biafra fighting until its eventual collapse in 1970.

"A war of attrition can dismantle a young state piece by piece — but it takes more than one lost city to break its will to fight." — KEY LESSON, THE BRAINSTORM HUB
